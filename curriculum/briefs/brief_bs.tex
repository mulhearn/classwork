\section*{Summary}

\noindent
In this section we present a summary of our proposal to revise the
undergraduate {\bf BS Physics} major (including the Astrophysics
specialization).  Further details are included in the sections that
follow.  We have also provided, along with this brief, a longer
write-up that describes our complete plan for revising the curriculum,
including the AB in Physics and BS in Applied Physics majors.\\[5pt]

\noindent
This proposal modifies the BS Physics major requirements in several
complementary ways, mostly aimed at improving student instruction,
informed by our experience with the current program and observed
shortcomings.  This proposal removes the requirement for a fifth
quarter in the honors introductory physics sequence (PHY 9HE), which
will be discontinued.  This will allow students following this
sequence to start on other requirements earlier.  The proposal updates
the math requirements to include additional new options.  The newer
math courses include all of the material needed by our majors, and are
already being accepted as equivalent on an ad-hoc basis.  This
proposal simply makes this policy explicit.  The Electricity and
Magnetism sequence has (PHY 110ABC) has been reduced to a two quarter
sequence (PHY 110AB) by removing some material and moving prerequisites
(such as vector calculus) to PHY 104A.  The requirement for PHY 102
(one unit) has been replaced with PHY 40 (three units).  Experience
has shown that it is not feasible to introduce computational physics
with one unit.  PHY 40 is a completely redesigned course, aimed to
introduce computational physics, with no physics or math
prerequisites, and has been assigned an appropriate number of
units.\\[5pt]

\noindent
The updated BS Physics major adds additional computational physics
requirements (PHY 45 and one unit computational physics labs PHY 110L,
112L, and 115L).  Computational physics continues to grow in
importance in our field.  The physics instrumentation lab sequence
(PHY 116ABC) has significant overlap with the newly available
experimental methods class (PHY 80) originally designed to better
prepare students for the senior lab courses (PHY 122A or PHY 122B).
This proposal replaces the requirement for PHY 116A, 116B, and 116C
with PHY 80, PHY 117, and PHY 118.  This teaches the same content, but
in a more efficient manner for both students and faculty.\\[5pt]

\noindent
The updated BS Physics major with astrophysics emphasis replaces the
PHY 116A elective option with the newly available options PHY 117 and
118 (either of which can be taken independently of the other).  The
newly available Galaxy Formation course (PHY 158) has been added as an
additional elective.\\[5pt]

\noindent
The proposed BS Physics major requirements can be completed with a
minimum of 110 units (from 107 units).  The proposed BS Physics with
astrophysics emphasis major requirements can be completed with a
minimum of 107 units (from 108 units).  Both changes are due mainly to
changes in computational physics requirements, but there are several
subtleties discussed below.

\section*{2. Background and Rationale}

\noindent
{\bf Prompt a:} {\it Explain all proposed revisions to the currently approved program.}\\[4pt]

\noindent
The updated BS Physics major requirements include the following changes:
\begin{enumerate}
\item PHY 9HE is removed from the Honors Physics sequence, and the
  course will be discontinued.
\item The math requirements have been updated to include additional
  new options available.
\item The computational physics course requirements have been updated.
  The previous requirement (PHY 102 or PHY 104B) has been replaced
  by the new required course PHY 45 (Computational Physics).  Students are also
  required to take three one-unit computational lab courses taught
  alongside core physics courses: PHY 110L, 112L, 115L.
\item PHY 110C is removed, and the course will be discontinued.
\item The lab sequence option PHY 116ABC has been replaced by required courses PHY 80
  and new courses PHY 117 and 118.
\end{enumerate}
The updated Astrophysics emphasis requirements include the following changes:
\begin{enumerate}
\item PHY 9HE is removed from the Honors Physics sequence, and the
  course will be discontinued.
\item The math requirements have been updated to include additional
  new options available.
\item PHY 110C is no longer an elective, as the course will be discontinued.
\item The PHY 102 (Computational Laboratory in Physics) requirement is
  eliminated.  PHY 40 is already required in the current requirements
  and will continue to be required.
\item The PHY 116A elective has been replaced with PHY 117 and 118 electives.
\item PHY 158 (Galaxy Formation) has been added as an option along
  with PHY 151,152,153, and 156.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent
{\bf Prompt b.} {\it Explain the rationale for why the proposed revisions are necessary.}\\[4pt]

\noindent
The changes proposed here address observed defects in our current program:
\begin{enumerate}
\item  Student workload over four years of study was imbalanced, with too few
  physics courses in the sophomore year, and too many in the junior
  year.
\item Integration between transfer students and four-year students,
  who have somewhat different backgrounds, needed to be improved. Our
  recent departmental Climate Survey confirmed this by showing a huge
  satisfaction gap between these groups.
\item The curriculum did not reflect the explosive growth in
  computational methods and their applications.
\item The highly successful new lab course PHY 80 introduced in a
  previous program revision overlapped significantly with lab courses
  PHY 116A and 116C.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent
Combined with already implemented schedule changes for other course
offerings, eliminating the PHY 9HE requirement allows students following
the honors physics sequence to start on other requirements starting in
the winter quarter of the their sophomore year.  This allows them to
balance their physics requirements more evenly throughout their four
years.  An additional benefit of the already implemented schedule changes,
including twice per year offerings of PHY 104A (Introduction to
Mathematical Methods in Physics), is that the course load for
transfer students is more balanced as well.\\

\noindent
Eliminating the PHY 110C requirement for BS majors allows us to add
additional required computational physics units while remaining under
the L\&S 110 unit cap on required coursework.  The content of PHY 110A
and 110B has already been modified.  PHY 104A now includes a review of
vector calculus that was formerly part of 110A.  PHY 110C is also
eliminated as an elective options for majors with an Astrophysics
emphasis.\\

\noindent
PHY 102 (Computational Lab in Physics) was technically a one-unit
course, but it was generally taught more like a 3-4 unit course.  Even
with the addition (in a previous program revision) of PHY 40, it is
not possible to cover computational physics in a meaningful way as a
single one unit course.  For BS Physics majors, the proposal keeps the
PHY 40 requirement, eliminates PHY 102, and adds new required courses
(PHY 45, 110L, 112L, 115L).  The PHY 102 requirement is eliminated for
majors with an Astrophysics emphasis as well.  Astrophysics students
continue to take PHY 40 and encounter additional computational physics
in their upper division course work (e.g. PHY 151, 152, 153, 156, and
158).\\

\noindent
PHY 80 (Experimental Techniques) was added as required course for all
majors in a previous revision, primarily to ensure that students were
adequately prepared for the advanced lab courses PHY 122A and 122B.
The course has been quite successful, however, there is significant
overlap with 116ABC.  The courses have been reorganized so that the
content in 116ABC is now contained in PHY 80, 117, and 118.\\

\noindent
We have been approving students requests to substitute newly available
math courses (MAT 27A, 67, 27B) where appropriate for our current
requirements (MAT 22A and 22B).  This proposal makes these
equivalencies explicit.\\

\noindent
Previously, majors with an Astrophysics emphasis were required to take
PHY 151, 152, 153, and 156.  In this proposal, students are given an
additional option, as they choose four courses from that list of four
plus the new course PHY 158 (Galaxy Formation).\\

\noindent
{\bf Prompt c.} {\it Describe the timing of and implementation plan for the revisions. Please be sure to address all impacts to students and catalog rights, including:}\\
{\bf i.} {\it How many existing students in the major will be affected by the proposed change?}\\
{\bf ii.} {\it Are any mitigation measures needed to prevent delays in time to degree for students already enrolled in the major or minor?}\\

\noindent
All of our approximately 150 physics BS majors will be affected by the
proposed changes.  However, all of the new courses have been taught
for several years now, and students have been advised to take them.
The necessary changes to course content and prerequisites in existing
courses have already been completed and approved in IPA.  We have also
already made the required adjustments to course scheduling.  Our
undergraduate advisor confirms that all current students will be able
to graduate under the new requirements without delaying their
graduation date.  Approving the new major requirements will clarify
the situation for our students, who have been hearing about these
changes for years, and so making the change as soon as possible would
be best for everyone.\\[5pt]

\noindent
There is a clear equivalency of requirements that will allow us to
handle special cases as needed:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
Old Requirement & New Requirement \\
\hline
PHY 9HE & Dropped \\
PHY 110C & Dropped \\
PHY 102 & PHY 40 \\
PHY 104B & PHY 45, 110L, 112L, 115L \\
PHY 116A, 116B, 116C & PHY 80, 117, 118 \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
We are also willing to waive the one-unit lab course requirements
during the transition on a case-by-case basis, in case we find that it
is necessary.  However, we suspect that it will not be necessary.\\

\section*{Required Units}

In the UC Davis course catalog, the minimum units required for a BS
physics major is listed as 101 units, but this is calculated from a
naive sum of the minimum in each sub-category, without accounting for
correlations.  Students that take PHY 122A or 122B to complete the lab
requirements at the minimum of four units are then required to take
two additional electives at a minimum of 6 units, which is not
accounted for in the catalog tally.  {\bf The actual minimum number of
  units required to complete the current BS physics requirements is
  107 units, despite what the catalog claims.} \\[5pt]

\noindent
This proposal increases this minimum to 109 units, due to the addition
of six units of new computational physics only partially compensated
by other changes.  Here's an accounting of which changes impact the
minimum, which nets three additional units relative to current
requirements:
\begin{itemize}
\item Drop PHY 102 (-1)
\item Add PHY 45, 110L, 112L, 115L (+7)
\item Reduce required upper-division three-or-more-unit coursework to 14 from 15 three (-3)
\item Drop PHY 110C, which is replaced by an upper-division elective (-1)
\end{itemize}
Despite the increase in two units of required coursework, we do not
expect actual student workload to increase, because the poorly
designed PHY 102 course, which worked students too hard for one unit,
is being eliminated.  Also, the amount of elective coursework
available to students has also not been diminished, because PHY 110C
has been eliminated.\\[5pt]

\noindent
In the UC Davis course catalog, the minimum units required for a BS
Physics with astrophysics specialization major is listed as 107 units.
However, this is only possible if students can take an additional
three unit elective, in addition to PHY 108 and 198.  There is no such
elective.  EPS 163 is a three unit course, but it includes additional
prerequisites.  {\bf The actual minimum number of units required to
  complete the current BS Physics with astrophysics specialization
  major requirements is 108 units, despite what the catalog claims.}
The new requirements eliminate PHY 102 (one unit) from the required
courses, which reduces the minimum number of units to 107.  Too
summarize, the minimum number of required units currently listed in
the catalog (107) is incorrect for the current requirements, but, by
coincidence only, is correct for the proposed requirements.\\[5pt]

\noindent
{\bf The physics department stands behind the minimum unit
  calculations presented here, and in our full proposal, as we have
  had independent confirmation of our tallies several times now.}
These unit minimums are surprisingly challenging to calculate
correctly.  A more detailed tally of required units under both the
current and proposed requirements is available in Appendix C of the
longer write-up.
