\noindent
\textbf {Course Description:}\\
Applications of computational physics to problems from Statistical
Mechanics.  Application of numerical techniques, and comparison to
analytic solutions (where they exist).\\[5pt]

\noindent
\textbf {Course Structure:}\\
The course runs on a two-week cycle.  In the first week of each cycle,
the previous assignment is due and the next assignment is described
during lecture.  In the second week, the lecture period begins with a
short in-class quiz on the assignment just submitted, and the
remaining time is available for questions and help with the current
assignment.  The last part of the quarter is devoted to the final
project.\\[5pt]

\noindent
\textbf {Course Philosophy:}\\
Computational physics is essential to nearly every career related to
physics.  Unfortunately, the educational pipeline, since grade school,
does not adequately prepare students with programming skills, and so a
central feature of computational physics courses is a wide
distribution of student programming abilities.  There are students
that are better programmers than most physics professors, students
that have little practice outside of the required computational
courses, and everything in between.\\[3pt]

\noindent
Fortunately, in this course we are concerned with {\bf computational
  physics} and not just {\bf computer programming}.  You do not need
to be an expert computer programmer to succeed in this course. You do
need to gain enough programming competency to handle the fascinating
physics problems we will tackle.  {\bf The primary objective of this
  course is to make you confident in using programming independently
  as an essential and effective tool for solving physics
  problems.}\\[3pt]

\noindent
This is a one unit course, and I will do my best to keep the workload
appropriate.  However, given the wide variation in student programming
abilities, inevitably some students will find the homework more
time-consuming than others.  If you do find yourself working longer on
the assignments, please understand that this is time well-spent:
gaining more proficiency in computing is almost certainly one of the
best possible uses of your time at this stage in your career.  There
are a vanishingly small number of physics-related career options that
do not require programming ability.\\[5pt]

\noindent
\textbf {Use of AI and Other External Resources:}\\
You must adhere to the UC Davis Code of Academic Conduct, available
online at the OSSJA website. In particular, you may not take credit for
any work not created by you.  {\bf Use of generative AI tools in this
  course is forbidden unless explicitly allowed below.}  Likewise, you
may not direct search engines, homework help websites, or other
external resources toward the specific problems that are assigned to
you.\\[3pt]

\noindent
The following uses of AI are allowed:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=0pt]
\item {\bf Learning Python.} You may use AI to learn or review general
  features of Python and its libraries (e.g.\ how \texttt{numpy}
  arrays or \texttt{matplotlib} plots work).  You may not give an AI
  any part of an assignment's text or your own assignment code, or ask
  it to write, debug, or explain code for an assignment.
\item {\bf Final project.} There are no restrictions on AI use for the
  final project.
\end{itemize}
If you use AI (or other external resources) on an assignment in a way
not allowed above, you must disclose it, and the assignment will
receive no credit.  If a student I suspect is using AI does not
disclose it, I will refer them to OSSJA, and if they are found
responsible, they will fail the course.\\[3pt]

\noindent
You are allowed to collaborate with other students in the course,
including discussing specific problems in the homework, unless you
have reason to believe the students are using unauthorized methods.
The solution that you provide must be your own: each and every line.
In general, you should be able to explain why each line in your code
is there.  If you do not understand something in your solution because
you relied on trial and error, then you should state that in the
comments, including explaining any other things that you tried.  For
example:
\begin{verbatim}
# I don't understand why this factor of two is needed
# I have included it because it gives the correct result:
\end{verbatim}

\noindent
\textbf {System of Units:}\\
To receive any credit, your solutions must adopt the system of units
as presented in lecture and in the write-up for each assignment.  The
system of units must be used throughout your solution.  You may not
e.g.\ do the calculation in SI and then convert to our system of units
later.  If you see physical constants like Boltzmann's constant or
Avogadro's number appearing in your code, that is likely a
misunderstanding. \\[5pt]

\noindent
\textbf{Homework and Quizzes:}\\
There will be four homework assignments featuring problems in
computational physics. Each assignment comes with a short checker
program, which you run in the last cell of your notebook before
submitting. It reports anything that is missing and whether your
results are correct to within the expected precision.  Try your best
to submit a notebook that passes the checker.  However, it is better
to submit an honest attempt than a fudged solution.  Before
submitting, restart your notebook and run every cell in order (Kernel
$\rightarrow$ Restart \& Run All), so that the output you submit is
the output of the code you submit.  Use the function names and the
reporting of results required by each assignment. Each assignment is
graded pass/fail\\[3pt]

\noindent
After you submit, your notebook is processed by automated tools,
including AI models that run only on a department computer under the
instructor's control: for example, to run additional tests, to
summarize your results, and to help prepare the questions for your
individual quiz.  {\bf Your work is never sent to any external or
  cloud-based AI service or other third party.}  All grading and quiz
decisions are made by the instructor and the TA, not by these
tools.\\[3pt]

\noindent
In the next lecture following the week an assignment was due, there
will be a short closed-notes quiz in class.  You should not need to
study for the quiz: its purpose is for you to show that you wrote, and
understand, the code that you submitted.  Most questions will be about
{\em your own} submitted notebook. As long as you wrote your own code
and understand it, you should be well prepared.  Failing the quiz
means failing the corresponding assignment.\\[3pt]

\noindent
{\bf Oral exam option:}\\
If you pass all for submitted assignments but
your course grade is failing due to quiz performance, you may request
an oral exam to demonstrate that the assignment work is your own.  If
you do, your course grade will be at least a C.  If not, your grade
stands.  Requests at least one week before the final project due date.
\\[5pt]

\noindent
\textbf{Final Project:}\\ In lieu of a final exam, you will complete
a final project.  I will provide a set of open-ended, deliberately
ambiguous questions.  You will choose one, build a numerical model
that illuminates it, and present your results.  You should be prepared
to explain and defend your modeling choices.  AI use is unrestricted
on the final project.  Further details will be provided in
lecture.\\[5pt]

\noindent
\textbf {Grades:}\\
Your final grade will be determined from the homework, the quizzes,
and the final project.  You will be allowed to submit one homework
assignment late, but you must complete every assignment and your final
project to complete the course.  You may also miss one quiz without
penalty (for example, the quiz for an assignment you submit late).\\[5pt]
