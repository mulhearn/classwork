{\bf Problem 3:}\\[5pt]
(A) Draw the lowest order Feynman diagram for the process:
$$e^+ + e^- \to \gamma \to u + \bar{u}.$$
Experimentally, at center-of-mass energies of around 10~\rm GeV, this
process produces two distinct jets of particles, the result of
hadronic showers of the final state $u$ and $\bar{u}$.  (Jump ahead to
Griffith's Fig 8.1 if you missed this in lecture.)\\[3pt]

\noindent
(B) Ignoring hadronization and focussing on just the Feynman diagram,
is this a QED process or a QCD process?\\[3pt]

\noindent
(C) Add a fundamental QCD vertex to your diagram from (A) for a
process that has {\bf three} jets in the final state.  Note that jets
originate from final state quarks or gluons.  There are two possible
places to attach the gluon: draw one diagram for each
possibility.\\[3pt]

\noindent
(D) Each QED vertex adds a factor of $\alpha_{\rm EM}$ to the rate of
the process, and each QCD vertex adds a factor of $\alpha_{\rm s}$.
There are other factors, but let's ignore them.  Assume each diagram
for a process contributes to the rate without any interference with
other diagrams.  With these assumptions, what would you expect the
ratio of the rate of process (C) to that of process (A) to be?  Hint:
the LEP experiment used these processes to measure $\alpha_s$.
